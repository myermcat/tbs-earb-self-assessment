# Talking to Nick and Allison

They run the assessor team that hand-builds the score decks today. This project changes
their job, so the conversation matters. Two rules for it.

**Ask what they do now, not what the tool should do.** They have never seen a tool like
this, so asking them to design it produces a description of the current template with
fewer slides. Ask about the work and the waste; design from that.

**Lead with what they stop doing.** The honest pitch is that they stop transcribing decks
into scores and start auditing the handful of answers that do not add up. That is a better
job. If it lands as "software is taking over your assessment", the project loses its two
most useful people.

## The questions worth asking

**How the work actually goes**

- Walk me through one recent assessment, start to finish. Where did the time go?
- Which part of it is worst? If one step disappeared tomorrow, which one?
- How much of building the summary deck is judgement, and how much is retyping?

**The corpus - this is the ask that matters most**

- Can we have the answer data from past assessments, or even twenty recent ones?
  Every dropdown in the tool has to come from real answers. Right now they are invented,
  and inventing them recreates exactly the problem Dan wants to kill.
- Is any of it already in a structured form, or is it all in the decks?

**Where their judgement lives**

- When you disagree with what a submitter claims, what tips you off?
- What does a padded answer look like? What does an honest low score look like?
- Which questions in the current template do you never trust the answer to?
- Which ones do you always skip?

**What the output is for**

- What goes in the summary deck that is not a score? Who reads that part?
- Who consumes the assessment besides the board?
- Does anyone ever go back to an old assessment? What for?

**The changes they will have opinions about**

- If a submitter self-scores above 60% and skips the board entirely, what would you need
  to see to be comfortable with that?
- Would you want to be able to change the weights, or is the rubric Dan's alone?
- How should submissions reach you - email, GCdocs, SharePoint, something else?

## What to show them, and what not to

Show the **reviewer side** first, not the questionnaire. Load a filled-in example, let them
see the triage list and the "Audit these" panel with the drafted challenge questions. That
is their new job, on screen, in about ninety seconds.

Do not show them the stand-in questions as though they were real. Say up front that the
content is a placeholder from Dan and the point is the machinery, or the whole conversation
becomes a critique of eighteen invented questions.
